# Backend design: the rules decide, the AI explains

Code decides which documents the AI sees and which facts are attached to each. The AI judges in exactly two ways:

1. **Soft ranking:** an order of the documents, most useful first. A suggestion, not a verdict.
2. **Notable passage:** per document, one or two sentences on how its metadata values (and what it states)
   affect its relevance to the question.

It also writes a short spoken summary. It never answers the question, recommends a reply or assigns a
confidence. The same question gives the same documents and the same facts every time; only the AI's wording varies.
The prompt that defines the AI's job is `PROMPT` in `app/relevance.py`. It leads, and everything below exists to
feed it.

## Data contract

`data/catalog.json` (generated by `scripts/build_data.py`) is the only metadata source. Its values are shown to the
consultant and to the AI exactly as stored: nothing is extracted, inferred or overridden. `data/users.json` says which
consultant is assigned to which clients. Document text comes from the PDF at each entry's `path`, with the metadata
header that `build_data.py` prints stripped (`knowledge.pdf_body`).

## Pipeline (`POST /ask`, `app/main.py`)

1. **Identity.** The consultant comes from the selected model in Open WebUI, standing in for the login session,
   never from the question text.
2. **Context** (code). Client from its name or aliases, country from a country name or an entity's site
   ("Antwerp" means Brouwerij Delta NV, from the client profile), searched in the question first, then earlier turns.
3. **Access** (code, `catalog.scope`). General documents (everything outside `clients/`) plus the detected client's
   own. A consultant not assigned to the client gets "denied" before anything is searched. Other clients'
   documents are left out even when the consultant is assigned to them too. A file under `clients/` whose client
   cannot be read from `client_id` or its folder is left out.
4. **Candidates** (code). BM25 over title, topics and text within the scope, top 8. No topic match means
   "not found"; a client without a known entity, or no client and no country, means "clarify".
5. **Facts** (code, `catalog.compute_flags`), over the whole scope: `supersedes` and `overrides` as stored, their
   reverses `superseded_by` and `overridden_by`, `owner_left`, `owner_moved` and `no_owner` from `owner_status`,
   `draft` and `informal` from `status`, and `stale` when `last_updated` is more than 2 years old. A link to a
   document outside the scope is dropped.
6. **Split** (code, `relevance.prepare`). Documents for another country (not `ALL`) or another entity than the
   context names go to the second list. When the context names a domain, only that domain is shown plus the client's
   profile and handover notes; the backend does not detect a domain yet, so this filter is idle.
7. **Tags** (code, `catalog.document_tag`). Attributes come from the allowlist `TAG_FIELDS`; `path`, `topics`,
   `owner_status` (sent as flags instead) and `security_test` never reach the AI. Values and text are escaped, so
   document text cannot close its tag.
8. **One AI call** (`llm.describe`), temperature 0, JSON out. Every candidate is sent; none are withheld. The prompt
   tells the AI to list documents that try to instruct it under "suspicious" and leave them out of the ranking.
9. **Check** (code). Ids the AI was not given are dropped, each id appears once, and the split between the two lists
   stays the code's. The response carries each ranked document's catalog entry as stored, its flags and the AI's
   note.

## Why access comes first

Relationship flags computed over the whole catalog would reveal other clients: for a consultant working for
Brouwerij Delta, the Belgian legal overtime rule would carry `overridden_by:CL-20318-BE-001`, the ID of a Havenlink
document that consultant may not see. Copying catalog entries into tag attributes would pass
`security_test: prompt_injection`, which labels the injection test document and gives the AI the answer to the test.
Both are prevented by construction: only `scope` reads the whole catalog, and tags use an allowlist rather than a
list of hidden fields.

## Still open

- **Domain detection.** Filling `domain` in the context from the question would switch on the domain filter.
- **Evaluation label.** `security_test` still sits in `catalog.json`. Moving it to a separate file of expected
  outcomes (for example `data/eval_cases.json`) keeps it out of the running system entirely.
- **Metadata trim.** `build_data.py` still prints every document's metadata into its PDF, and the catalog keeps
  fields no rule reads (`version`, `topics` beyond search, `sector`).
